# Music

`lantern-gospel-06` is an original 32-second, wordless sacred choir. Its D-major centre, added sixths and ninths, common-tone voice leading and soft G-to-D plagal return favour warmth over threat. One relative-minor phrase adds shade. The earlier Phrygian semitone, altered dominant and descending dark bass have been removed. The design is a spacious festival surrounding one solitary passenger, without a march or drum groove.

Each of the six parts now has an independent note sequence, rests and phrase lengths. Alto and mezzo calls receive soprano answers; the lead changes at different points, with a mezzo/soprano co-lead in the final section. Inner parts hold common tones or move against the lead. Passing tones resolve by step. Several sustained phrases cross the loop boundary. This is gospel-inspired call-and-response, not strict species counterpoint.

Six parts contain four separately synthesized singers each. Five female-led sections cover velvet contralto, warm alto, round mezzo and two contrasting sopranos; one quiet male section supports them. The small male section sits around D3; the female sections have separately written registers and melodies, while the organ retains the low foundation. A differentiated glottal-flow cycle excites five vocal-tract resonances per singer. Tenor, alto, interpolated mezzo and soprano vowel parameters differ; individual tract length, pitch drift, vibrato and breathing also differ. Each section opens its vowel differently from O toward A as the choir swells. Softer source closures, individual low-pass filters and reduced vibrato keep the blend gentle. These are synthetic vowel voices, not recordings or intelligible Latin lyrics.

The two sides and three tiers of swans map to the six voice groups. Every bird within a gallery shares one shape and one pose, driven by the corresponding vocal part. The lower choir uses rounder silhouettes and slow, broad strokes; the middle choir uses full strokes at note entries; the slender upper choir uses paired response strokes. Rests fold the wings. Lead and supporting roles control the gesture strength. Lead voices are drier than the supporting choir so faster lines remain distinct. The duck's small calls stay close to the stereo centre; the massed choir occupies the sides and the reverberant hall. A reduced low pedal and quiet, consonant bells provide scale without a drum groove.

Persistent oscillators receive a sub-cent integrated-phase correction. Three laps are rendered with a finite 7.2-second room response, then the settled middle lap is retained. Pausing and seeking use the same scene clock as the visuals.

The per-bird individuality control has been removed. No random body proportions, head turns, timing offsets or independent hopping remain. Three fixed silhouettes identify the vocal families.
